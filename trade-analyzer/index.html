How to evaluate a 9-cat trade

Count category by category

In 9-cat head to head you win categories, not points. Start from the totals of each side in every category. A trade that wins five categories by a little can help more than one that wins two by a lot.

Percentages are weighted

FG% and FT% are not averages of player percentages. They come from total makes and attempts, so a high-volume shooter moves your team percentage much more than a player who rarely shoots. Look at how your team percentage changes, not only at the players.

Minutes and role

Stats follow minutes. Compare both players over the same period, the whole season or the last 15 days, and check whether a role is growing or shrinking. A player whose minutes just went up can be worth more than his season averages show.

Games played and health

A player who misses games does not help your weekly totals. Check how many games each player has played and the latest injury news before you accept.

2-for-1 trades and your roster spot

When you get two players for one, someone has to leave your roster, so count that player as part of the trade. When you get one player for two, you open a spot for the waiver wire, and that spot has value too.

Punting

If you punt a category, take it out of the comparison. A trade that only loses your punted categories can be a clear win for your build.

Your team, not a vacuum

The same trade can be good for one team and bad for another. Gains in categories where you are weak matter more than gains where you already lead your league.

Frequently asked questions

How are FG% and FT% calculated?

From the total makes and attempts of all players on each side, not by averaging percentages. A player who shoots 60% on 3 attempts moves the result less than one who shoots 50% on 15 attempts. With your roster added, the tool also shows how much your team percentage changes.

What is 9-cat?

9-cat is the most common category format in fantasy basketball: points, rebounds, assists, three pointers, steals, blocks, field goal percentage, free throw percentage and turnovers. In head to head leagues you win or lose each category every week. For turnovers, fewer is better.

What does "low volume" mean?

A percentage built on very few attempts, under 3 field goal or 1.5 free throw attempts per game on one side. It barely changes a team percentage, so when the trade does not change your saved roster, that category counts as a tie in the score. With your roster added, a category also counts as a tie when your team percentage changes by less than .001.
